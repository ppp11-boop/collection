My subject is one toilet-paper roll in my bathroom. It disappears in the most ordinary way: each use removes part of it until the paper is gone and only a cardboard tube, and then an empty holder, remains. I chose it because its disappearance is so usual but often been forgotten. Most people only notice when a toilet roll only, but while it is slowly becoming empty.

The twenty records share the heading "Evidence of Use." Together they argue that an everyday object can leave visible and audible evidence of being consumed, even when the actions causing that consumption remain private. Photographs record how the tissue is being used slowly. Sound records movements and tearing. Text records the texture of the tissue.

The collection cannot hold the whole life of the roll. It does not preserve touch, smell, bodily sensation, intention or the complete context of each use. It also avoids recording the private acts for which the tissue was used. The `not_captured` fields are therefore not minor omissions. They mark the boundary between evidence and experience. Even when the final record shows an empty space, it cannot contain everything that caused the roll to vanish.
