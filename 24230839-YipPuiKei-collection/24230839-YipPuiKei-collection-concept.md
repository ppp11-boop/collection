# Evidence of Use: Twenty Encounters with a Disappearing Toilet Roll

My subject is one toilet-paper roll in my bathroom. It disappears in the most ordinary way: each use removes part of it until the paper is gone and only a cardboard tube, and then an empty holder, remains. I chose it because its disappearance is predictable but usually ignored. Most people notice a toilet roll only when it is empty, not while it is slowly becoming empty.

The twenty records share the heading "Evidence of Use." Together they argue that an everyday object can leave visible and audible evidence of being consumed, even when the actions causing that consumption remain private. The sequence moves from presence to reduction, fragment and absence. Photographs record changes in size, surface, edge, light and shadow. Sound records movement and tearing. Text records measurements and a personal encounter. Arranged in this order, the records do more than identify the object: they show disappearance as a process made from many small events.

The collection cannot hold the whole life of the roll. It does not preserve touch, smell, bodily sensation, intention or the complete context of each use. It also avoids recording the private acts for which the tissue was used. The `not_captured` fields are therefore not minor omissions. They mark the boundary between evidence and experience. Even when the final record shows an empty space, it cannot contain everything that caused the roll to vanish.
